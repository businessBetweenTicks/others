It works because she lets it

Motion and tone

The whole approach, in one line
Write it as a sentence she says, not a system she uses — each thing she shares set directly beside the chase it removes, so the section reads like a receipt of what she gets back, not a privacy policy.
Why it is built this way
A privacy section fails when it argues. The moment a page starts reassuring you about safety, it has told you there is something to be afraid of — and a mother two weeks postpartum has neither the attention nor the patience for a defence. So this section never defends anything. It shows a trade, in two rows, and lets her do the arithmetic herself.
Her side of the trade is in the serif, in the first person, because the serif is her voice everywhere else on the site. What it takes off her is in the sans, because the sans is the work. The reader is never told that split exists; by this section they have felt it three times already.
Sequence · triggered once when the section is 40% in view
1
Heading, then the paragraph
Opacity 0 → 1, y +14px → 0, 620ms on ease-rise, 130ms apart. The eyebrow goes first, alone, 220ms ahead of the heading.
2
The two rows of the trade
Each row's hairline draws left to right over 620ms on ease-settle — a rule being ruled, not a bar filling. Her line fades up as the rule passes under it; what it takes off her follows 220ms later, so the reader always reads the giving before the getting. Rows are 130ms apart.
3
The two lines becoming one
The two dots fade in together, 420ms on ease-soft. Then each curve draws on stroke-dashoffset, 900ms on ease-settle, the second starting 220ms after the first, so they arrive at the join a beat apart rather than together. The moss dot draws last, 420ms. The bubble arrives 220ms after that, 620ms on ease-rise. Nothing pulses, nothing travels along the line, and the line never animates again once drawn.
4
The three lines that close it
620ms each on ease-rise, 130ms apart, and then nothing. “She can disconnect any time” is the last thing that moves in the section, which is the point of putting it last.
About 4.2s end to end. Same three easing curves and four durations as the hero — this section introduces no new motion vocabulary, because a section about trust is the worst possible place to start behaving differently.
Not scroll-scrubbed
The brief says “drawn as it scrolls in,” and the obvious reading is to tie the draw to scroll position. Don't. Scrubbed motion runs at the reader's scroll speed, so a fast flick makes the calmest diagram on the site snap, and a slow one makes it stutter. It plays once on entry, at its own pace, and finishes whether she keeps scrolling or not.
Never, in this section above all
No padlock, shield, key or fingerprint.
No server, cloud, node or network diagram.
No “encrypted”, “secure”, “bank-level”, “compliant”.
No toggles or switches — this is a page, not settings.
No trust badges, seals, or percentages.
No green. Moss appears once, on the join.
No arrowheads on the curves — nothing is being sent.
No second colour. The diagram is one hairline weight.
Every one of those is the reflex move for a data section, and every one of them announces risk. The diagram has no arrowheads for the same reason the copy has no reassurance: nothing is travelling anywhere, so nothing needs to be defended.
Reduced motion, and the copy under it
prefers-reduced-motion: reduce — the section renders complete, curves already drawn. It loses nothing, because the diagram was never carrying the argument; the two rows were.
Text alternative — the diagram is described as: “Her delivery record and her insurance become one thread.” The rows need none; they are already text.
One copy flag for the client. The lead paragraph is the brief's, verbatim, including “who her plan covers.” The packages sheet lists plan as a word to avoid, in the subscription sense — here it plainly means her insurance, so it reads clean, but if it should go, “who her insurance covers” is a one-word swap that costs nothing.
