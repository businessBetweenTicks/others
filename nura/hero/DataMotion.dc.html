It works because she lets it

How this section earns trust

The approach, in one line
Answer the question instead of reassuring her about it — three plain steps for what she shares and what it buys her, then a line with the people who never see any of it named on the far side.
Why it is built this way
A trust section fails when it argues. The moment a page starts reassuring you about safety it has told you there is something to fear, and a mother two weeks postpartum has neither the attention nor the patience for a defence. So this section makes no case at all. It answers three questions in order — who decides, what is read, what does she get — and then names, in plain words, the people who never see any of it.
Nothing is abstracted. Step 02 shows the two records as two chips, because two is the whole list and a short list of real things is more reassuring than any assurance about it. Step 03 shows what came back as the actual messages, in the same bubbles the hero uses, because a description of a benefit is worth less than the benefit itself sitting on the page.
The hardest sentence in the brief is “not with the giver.” The person reading this page is the giver, and they are about to spend money. Telling them plainly that they will never see any of it is the single most trust-building thing the section does, so it is set as its own block rather than buried in a paragraph.

About 5.4s end to end. Same three easing curves and four durations as the hero — this section introduces no new motion vocabulary, because a section about trust is the worst possible place to start behaving differently.
Not scroll-scrubbed
The brief says “drawn as it scrolls in,” and the obvious reading is to tie the draw to scroll position. Don't. Scrubbed motion runs at the reader's scroll speed, so a fast flick makes the calmest moment on the site snap and a slow one makes it stutter. It plays once on entry, at its own pace, and finishes whether she keeps scrolling or not.
Never, in this section above all
No padlock, shield, key or fingerprint.
No server, cloud, node or network diagram.
No “encrypted”, “secure”, “bank-level”, “compliant”.
No toggles or switches — this is a page, not settings.
No trust badges, seals, or percentages.
No red, and no warning tone on the far column.
No crosses or strike-throughs on the three names.
No green. Moss appears only on what stays hers.
Every one of those is the reflex move for a data section, and every one announces risk. The three names get a quiet dash, not a cross: a cross says something was refused, a dash says it was simply never on the list.
Reduced motion, and one flag for the client
prefers-reduced-motion: reduce — the section renders complete, rules already drawn. It loses nothing: the order was a courtesy to the reader, not the argument. The argument is the words and the line, and both are there in the still.
Screen readers — the three steps are a numbered list, and the boundary is two lists with their headings read out: “Stays with her”, three items; “Never sees any of it”, three items. No part of the meaning lives in the dashed line alone.
Copy flag. Step 03 uses the brief's “who her plan covers.” The packages sheet lists plan as a word to avoid in its subscription sense; here it plainly means her insurance, so it reads clean, but “her insurance covers” is a one-word swap if it should go.
