Nura · consumer gift site

Photography, art direction

Three frames carry the whole site. They are commissioned, not stocked — every stock library's "new mother" is the thing this brand exists to be the opposite of. Below is what to shoot, how to light it, and where each frame lands.

She is the subject. The baby is never in frame.
Every other rule on this page follows from that one. If a frame would still work on a nursery brand's site, it is the wrong frame.
The shot list · three frames, in priority order
01
Square
1:1 · hero stage
Her, at the window
Early thirties, seated near a large window in late afternoon. The wrap over her shoulders, both hands around a plain ceramic mug. She is looking away, out of frame — not at the camera, not at anything in particular. Tired and unbothered by it. She occupies the left third; the right half of the frame is quiet, empty room.
Why the empty right half: the resolved cards sit there in the hero composition. The negative space is not styling, it is layout — shoot it or the hero does not work.
02
4:5 portrait
“Something to hold”
The wrap, as an object
The wrap folded, or held in two hands against a plain surface. Close enough to read the weave and the stitched name inside the hem. One frame of the box open — wrap and card inside, nothing else. Warm light, no props, no baby in frame, no nursery in the background.
This is the only thing you can hold, so it has to be photographed like a considered object — the way a good knitwear brand shoots a coat, not the way a baby brand shoots a blanket.
03
3:2 landscape
Storyboard 07 · “Out the door”
The walk
Her, from behind or three-quarters, walking. Coat or vest, late afternoon, a real street or a park path with bare trees. No pram, no sling, no companion. She is going somewhere on her own for twenty minutes, and the frame should feel like relief rather than achievement.
Optional second plate in the hero animation — storyboard frame 07 cross-dissolves to it over 900ms. If only one frame is licensed, hold frame 01 and the sequence loses nothing.
Light and grade
Source
One window, late afternoon, from camera left. No fill, no reflector, no strobe. The shadow side stays dark.
Lens
50mm, wide open. Shallow enough that the room falls away, never so shallow it looks like a portrait session.
Grade
Warm and desaturated. Black point lifted slightly so nothing is truly black. Clamp the palette to oat, clay and warm off-white — the same family as the page.
Grain
Fine 35mm, matched to the 5.5% grain already sitting on the hero stage so the photograph and the page share one surface.

Clinical or hospital settings
Casting: one woman, real postpartum body, minimal hair and make-up, own clothes under the wrap. Shoot in a real home. The moment we art-direct her into looking rested, the site is lying and the reader knows it.

Deliver 2048px on the long edge, sRGB, uncropped masters plus the four crops above. Images sit flush in a 2px-radius plate with no border and no overlay text, ever.
Interim comp
Until shot 01 exists, the hero stage is composed to stand without it — window light, grain and a warm field, with the thread bleeding off the bottom edge. That is a real state, not a placeholder: it is what ships if the shoot slips, and it is what renders behind the photograph while the image loads.
