Nura — gift site
Submission
The one line you asked for
A trust section fails the moment it starts reassuring you — so I would make it answer instead: what she shares, what she gets back, and who never sees it, with the giver named on the far side of that line, because the person reading the page is the giver, and telling them plainly is the most reassuring thing on it.
Why that line
Reassurance is what you reach for when you cannot answer. Say your data is safe with us and you have told the reader there is something to be unsafe about. A mother two weeks postpartum has neither the attention nor the patience for a defence, so the section makes no case at all. It answers three questions in order — who decides, what is read, what does she get — and stops.
Nothing is abstracted, because abstraction is what makes a trust section feel technical. Step 02 shows the two records as two chips, since two is the entire list and a short list of real things reassures more than any sentence about safety. Step 03 shows what came back as the actual messages, in the same bubbles the hero uses, because a description of a benefit is worth less than the benefit sitting on the page.
Then the part your brief makes hardest. Not with the giver is the sentence the page has to survive, because the person reading it is the giver and they are about to spend money. It gets its own block with a line down the middle and three names on the far side — the giver, her doctor, anyone else — carrying a quiet dash, never a cross. A cross says something was refused. A dash says it was never on the list. In the motion the line finishes drawing before those names appear: the barrier exists before the people do. Reverse that order and the section undoes itself.

Tool
Figma plus HTML and CSS. The layouts are generated from one shared token set, which is why four breakpoints of nine sections stay consistent and why a change to a colour or a type step lands everywhere at once. Handover is the Figma file with layers named, the style set as shared styles, and the four breakpoints as frames.
Motion is specified, not built: curves, durations, stagger, trigger points and the reduced-motion fallback for every section, written so a developer can implement it without asking a question.

Read closely, and flagged
The First Week Check scene is not a testimonial. Nura has not launched, so there is no mother whose week that was. It is set in the first person because that is how it is lived, and labelled so — not in her words. When there are real accounts they will run there instead, with her name on them. If there is already a real one, tell me and it becomes stronger, not weaker.
Three cuts from the First Week Check document, on your own instruction that vendors, pricing math and clinical plans travel no further: the named county programme and its rollout dates, the weight-loss thresholds and the fax protocol, and the competitor-rates passage. All three are named in the file rather than quietly dropped, because the last one is the passage most likely to reach a public page by mistake.
One word kept against the rules, on purpose. App appears twice, both in her voice and both negations — not an app, just a text. Naming what Nura is not is the positioning the whole site rests on. The same test removed wallet from care balance, where the sentence survived without it.
One product question the design cannot settle. Step 2 of the flow opens a shareable pool before payment. If a pool can exist before the first giver has paid, the share control belongs there as drawn; if it cannot, it moves to the confirmation screen. The layout survives either, but you should decide it, not me.
Still open
No photography exists yet, so the hero and the physical gift are composed to stand without it — light, grain and a crop rather than a placeholder box. The shoot is art-directed in the file: three shots, with the one constraint that decides the layout, that the right half of shot 01 must be empty room.
The nine she can change lines beyond your example, the three add-on prices, and the optional fields on step 4 are written to your instruction but are mine, and want a read-back. Package prices are yours, from the packages sheet.
The whole site has one job: to make a gift feel like relief rather than one more thing to manage.
Every decision in the file comes back to that. No dashboards, because a dashboard is a chore. No amounts, because an amount is a score. No progress bar on the care balance, because a bar implies she is behind. And nothing on the page that celebrates — somebody's friend has just had a baby and is not sleeping, so the tone at the end is relief, not confetti.
